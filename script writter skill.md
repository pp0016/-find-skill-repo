

### The Seven Laws of Scripting Million-View Videos

#### 1. Vein Theory
*   **The Concept:** Every video must follow **one clear narrative or storyline** from start to finish. A video is essentially a rolling clock or a "time bomb"; to stop viewers from scrolling away, you must capture them instantly and push them smoothly along this single "vein" of thought.
*   **The Rules:** 
    *   A video must focus on **one clear topic and solve one specific problem**. Avoid "multi-tip" hooks (e.g., "3 tips to overcome belly fat") because they pull the viewer in too many directions right at the start.
    *   Build the script like a **tower of Lego blocks**, where every line directly supports and builds upon the last. 
    *   Ruthlessly cut out any repetitions, deviations, or non-essential information. Every single word must **"fight for its place"** in the video.
    *   The primary blocks to a video's vein are bad hooks, undefined terms, excessive complexity, and irrelevant details. 

#### 2. The Demand Anchor
*   **The Concept:** You must anchor your video's hook to a concept that **your audience actually cares about**. Topic selection is the single most important factor in whether a video performs.
*   **The Rules:**
    *   Understand the **Pyramid of Demand**: Universal themes (like relationships, fitness, and health) sit at the bottom and interest the most people. Above these are broader cultural interests, followed by niche interests (like Pokémon cards), and finally microniche interests (like writing hooks) which naturally yield lower views.
    *   Make your hooks **observable**—based on situations people have actually seen or experienced in real life. Focus on real-life *symptoms* rather than abstract concepts (for example, discuss a local homeless situation rather than saying "politics is fucked").
    *   **Pre-validate your topics** before filming. You can do this by curating your own social feeds to spot high-performing concepts, searching Reddit forums for common user problems, or transcribing your sales calls to find the exact phrasing prospects repeat (e.g., "I don't feel like myself").

#### 3. Build Your Supply
*   **The Concept:** You must meet the audience's demand with your **genuine, unique expertise and skillset**. 
*   **The Rules:**
    *   Do not copy other creators' viral hooks if you lack the actual skill to solve that specific problem.
    *   **Avoid relying on AI** to write your scripts; because it pulls generic, recycled information from the internet, it results in mediocre videos that fail to perform.
    *   **Manually write out** how you would solve the problem based on your real-world experience.
    *   Develop and use **pre-built frameworks** (like Mitchell's "BITE" framework: Bait, Illustrate, Tail end) to make scripting faster, repeatable, and highly structured.

#### 4. The Logic Chain
*   **The Concept:** A video script should behave like a **series of falling dominoes**.
*   **The Rules:**
    *   Dissect your script line-by-line to ensure every single sentence flows flawlessly into the next with strict cause-and-effect congruence. 
    *   An example of a successful logic chain: *eating 100g of carbs $\rightarrow$ gains 4g of water $\rightarrow$ stores more glycogen $\rightarrow$ holds more water $\rightarrow$ scale goes up*.

#### 5. Defining Terms
*   **The Concept:** Jargon and undefined words act as major roadblocks that cause viewers to tune out.
*   **The Rules:**
    *   If a viewer ever has to ask, "What does that mean?", the script has failed.
    *   **Simplify and define every industry term** so simply that a complete beginner can understand it, whilst experts appreciate the sheer clarity.
    *   Instead of saying "progressive overload", explain the physical action: lift a weight for a set number of reps, add more weight once that becomes easy, and repeat. Instead of telling someone to "track macros", tell them to download MyFitnessPal, buy a food scale, weigh out 200g of chicken and rice, and eat it.

#### 6. Operationalising Action Items
*   **The Concept:** Viewers need to know **exactly what to do** with the information you have given them.
*   **The Rules:**
    *   At the end of your script, tell the audience exactly **what they should physically do with their hands**. 
    *   Break instructions down into dumber, simpler, and more basic sequential steps than you think are necessary, as viewers usually have very little context. For instance, tell someone exactly how to copy a competitor's outlier video titles step-by-step rather than just telling them to "grow their channel".

#### 7. Polish the Script
*   **The Concept:** Writing the first draft is only the beginning; you must actively refine and polish your work.
*   **The Rules:**
    *   Give your script at least **two to three thorough rereads**.
    *   **Read the script out loud** to ensure it matches the natural way you speak.
    *   Step away from the script for 30 minutes, an hour, or even a day. Return with fresh eyes to review it strictly from the perspective of a complete stranger who has never seen your face before, checking that the logic chain holds and all terms remain perfectly clear.
